Problem Definition

VocaDine Germany

Voice restaurant assistant for English-speaking tourists

Sarah, 28, arrives in Haigerloch. She speaks no German. She says: "Italian food for two, nothing too fancy" — the assistant asks two follow-ups and reads out the top 3 restaurants. End-to-end: ~7 seconds.

  • →

  • →
    Voice out: Microsoft Neural TTS (edge-tts, en-US-JennyNeural) — no API key required

NLU is layered: the trained spaCy model runs first on every utterance. Only slots it misses are passed to the rule layer. This means unseen phrasings ("I fancy sushi", "plant-based") are handled without a keyword entry.

Dialog Management

Implicit slot filling · validation · adaptive pacing

Input: "Italian food in Berlin for two tonight, nothing too fancy"
Extracted: location=Berlin · cuisine=Italian · group_size=2 · datetime=tonight · budget=moderate
Next question: "Any dietary restrictions?" (only remaining critical slot)
  • 1
    Implicit filling: up to 5 slots extracted per utterance — already-filled slots skipped automatically
  • 2
    Validation probe: after high-impact slot (location, cuisine, diet) → quick Places API call; 0 results → slot rolled back, re-prompted
  • 3
    Impatience detection: VADER compound < −0.5 OR "stop asking" → all remaining slots = "any", immediate search (Hutto & Gilbert, 2014)
  • 4
    Ambiguity resolution: ≥2 cuisines in one utterance → "I noticed Italian and Greek — which do you prefer?"
  • 5
    Diet inference: meat keyword in past_experience without negation → diet="none" inferred, question skipped

Training Data · Train NLP Models

NLU: spaCy NER Training

600 annotated utterances · 5 entity labels · blank English model

Why a trained model?

Rule-based matching achieves perfect scores on utterances it was designed for — but fails silently on unseen phrasings. A trained NER model generalises: "I fancy sushi", "plant-based options", "we're counting pennies" are recognised without a keyword entry.

Recommendation Mechanism

TF-IDF Recommendation Engine

scikit-learn · feature engineering · honest limitations

scoring formula
score = (cosine_sim × 0.4) + (rating/5.0 × 0.6)
      + budget_bonus  (+0.1 if priceRange matches)
      + attribute_bonus (+0.1 outdoor / groups)

Feature engineering

  • →
    Restaurant: name + types + editorialSummary + top-3 review texts + attribute tokens ("outdoor seating terrace", "good for groups")
  • →
    User: cuisine + diet + occasion + past_experience free text
  • →
    Why 40/60? Design decision: Google's crowd-sourced rating is assumed to correlate more reliably with overall quality than sparse keyword overlap on short descriptions
Known limitations: When Places API returns only generic types (e.g. "restaurant", "food"), TF-IDF degrades — reviews and editorial summary become the primary signal. TF-IDF cannot capture semantic similarity: "sushi" and "Japanese" are unrelated tokens unless co-occurring.
On the "training" claim: fit_transform() at runtime computes IDF weights from the live result set. This is lightweight on-demand vectoriser fitting — not equivalent to offline model training with a large corpus. The genuine ML training component is the spaCy NER model (Slide 5).
Rafailidis & Manolopoulos (2019): targeted ranking mechanisms produce significantly more relevant recommendations than popularity-only. Top 3 results: keeps response concise and actionable.
